The Operator Table

You have now met every operator. Which one goes first when they appear together? This looks like the hardest topic in Part 1 — but with one ladder, one sentence and three short rules, you will solve every exam question.

By the end of this lesson you can
  • explain precedence (who goes first) and associativity (who goes first on a tie)
  • recall the whole table from a picture and a one-line memory sentence
  • solve any expression with the 3-step method, and check it with a live solver
The problem

What does 2 + 3 * 4 equal?

Left to right?

(2 + 3) × 4 = 20

Multiplication first?

2 + (3 × 4) = 14

You already know the answer from maths class: 14. You use the rule BODMAS without thinking. C++ has the same idea, just with many more operators, so it needs a complete ranking: the operator table.

Picture it

A school has ranks: Principal, Teachers, Students. When two people give you orders, you obey the higher rank first. Operators have ranks too.

Two questions, two rules

Two ideas decide everything

1. Precedence — who goes first?

When different operators meet, the higher rank goes first.

2 + 3 * 4 → * outranks + → 2 + (3 * 4) = 14

2. Associativity — who wins a tie?

When operators of the same rank meet, direction decides: Left→Right or Right→Left.

10 - 4 - 3 → left to right → (10 - 4) - 3 = 3 (not 9!)

Remember

Precedence compares different operators.
Associativity breaks a tie between equal ones.

Try it

What is a = b = c = 5? Assignment is right to left: a = (b = (c = 5)). First c gets 5, then b gets c’s value, then a does. All three become 5.

Memory trick 2: inside the families

AND beats OR. Every time.

Math

* / % go before + -

Just like BODMAS.

Relational

< <= > >= go before == !=

“Compare sizes first, then check equality.”

Bitwise

& then ^ then |

AND → XOR → OR

Logical

! then && then ||

NOT → AND → OR

AND always comes before OR — in & vs | and in && vs ||
Famous trap

Bitwise operators rank below comparison. So 1 + 2 == 3 & 4 means ((1 + 2) == 3) & 4 = 1 & 4 = 0. When in doubt, use brackets.

Memory trick 3: direction

Only three families go right-to-left

Unary  ·  Ternary  ·  Assignment   →   R → L
Everything else is L → R

Unary

Has one operand, which sits on its right. !-x means !(-x): the nearest operator to x acts first.

Ternary

a ? b : c ? d : e means a ? b : (c ? d : e).

Assignment

The value flows leftwards into the variable. a = b = 5 means a = (b = 5).

Why it makes sense

Remember “U-T-A” like the name Uta. Unary, Ternary, Assignment all reach to the right for their input, so they resolve from the right.

The method

Solve any expression in 3 steps

  1. Step 1 — Add brackets using precedence

    Put brackets around the highest-ranked operator pairs first, then work down the ladder.

  2. Step 2 — Break ties with associativity

    Same rank? Group left-to-right, except unary, ternary and assignment (right-to-left).

  3. Step 3 — Evaluate the innermost brackets outward

    Now there is no doubt about the order. Just calculate.

Worked example: 2 + 3 * 4 - 6 / 2

1. * and / outrank + - → 2 + (3 * 4) - (6 / 2)

2. + and - tie, left to right → (2 + (3 * 4)) - (6 / 2)

3. (2 + 12) - 3 → 14 - 3 = 11

Another worked example

5 > 3 && 2 < 1 || 4 == 4

1. Precedence. Relational (>, <) and equality (==) outrank &&, which outranks ||:

((5 > 3) && (2 < 1)) || (4 == 4)

2. Evaluate the innermost brackets. 5 > 3 is true (1); 2 < 1 is false (0); 4 == 4 is true (1):

(1 && 0) || 1

3. AND before OR. 1 && 0 = 0, then 0 || 1 =

1  (true)

Reminder

In C++ every comparison gives 1 (true) or 0 (false), so you can keep calculating with the results.

A subtle point

Precedence tells you the grouping, not the timing

In f() + g(), the + is clear, but C++ does not promise whether f() or g() is called first.

❌ Avoid in real programs

i = i++ + ++i;
a[i] = i++;

Changing a variable twice in one statement gives undefined behaviour. Different compilers give different answers.

✅ What to do

Use brackets to make your meaning obvious, and change a variable once per statement.

In an exam, follow the rules in this lesson; in real code, keep it simple.

Lesson 7 recap

What we learned

✅ The three memory tools

  • Sentence: Big Uncles Make Sweet Rasgullas, Because Little Tinku Always Cries.
  • AND beats OR in both bitwise and logical.
  • U-T-A go right to left; everything else left to right.

✅ The method

  • 1) bracket by precedence
  • 2) break ties by associativity
  • 3) evaluate innermost first
  • When in doubt, use brackets!
